Getting started
What's the difference between a medspa and a day spa?

A medical spa operates under the license of a medical director — typically a physician or nurse practitioner — and is authorized to perform clinical procedures like Botox, dermal fillers, laser treatments, and prescription-strength skincare. A day spa offers services like facials, massage, and nail care that don't require a medical license.

At Rejuvalase, our medical director is directly involved in clinical oversight, all injectable treatments are performed by licensed registered nurses or nurse practitioners, and we follow strict protocols for patient safety, consent, and emergency preparedness. You get the relaxing environment of a spa with the standards of a medical practice.

Injectables
Is Botox safe?

Botulinum toxin type A — marketed as Botox, Dysport, Xeomin, and Jeuveau — has been FDA-approved for cosmetic use since 2002 and has one of the most extensive safety records of any pharmaceutical in aesthetic medicine. When administered in appropriate doses by a trained provider, serious adverse events are extremely rare.

The most common side effects — mild swelling, bruising, or temporary tenderness at injection sites — resolve within days. Eyelid ptosis (drooping) is possible but uncommon, and resolves on its own within weeks. We review your full medical history before any injectable treatment and discuss any specific contraindications relevant to you.

How long do results last?

Neuromodulators like Botox typically last 3–4 months for most patients, though first-time patients and those with highly active facial muscles may see results fade closer to 2.5 months. With consistent treatment over time, many patients find they can extend intervals to 4–5 months as the treated muscles become less active.

Hyaluronic acid dermal fillers vary significantly by product and placement — lighter lip fillers may last 6–9 months, while structural cheek fillers can persist for 12–18 months or longer. We'll give you realistic expectations based on the specific products we're using and the areas we're treating.

What's the difference between Botox and Dysport?

Both are botulinum toxin type A, but they're formulated differently. Dysport has a slightly smaller molecular size, which means it tends to diffuse a bit more broadly from the injection point — making it a strong choice for larger areas like the forehead. Botox stays more localized, which is often preferred for precision work around the eyes or lips.

The dosing is also different: Dysport units are not equivalent to Botox units (roughly 2.5–3 Dysport units per 1 Botox unit), so the number on your invoice can look different without meaning you're getting more or less treatment. Onset tends to be slightly faster with Dysport for some patients. We use both and will recommend what we think works best for your specific anatomy and goals.

How much does treatment cost?

Neuromodulator treatments are priced per unit, with a typical forehead and crow's-feet treatment running between $300–$500 depending on the number of units needed. Filler is priced per syringe, typically starting at $650, depending on the product. Full pricing is discussed at consultation — and we never add surprise charges after treatment begins.

We offer a loyalty program for returning patients and periodic specials on select treatments. What we don't do is push you toward more product than you need to hit a price point. If you'd be well-served by a half-syringe, that's what we'll recommend.

What if I don't like the result?

For neuromodulators, the treatment wears off naturally over 3–4 months, and in most cases minor asymmetries can be addressed with a small touch-up at your two-week follow-up appointment. Botox cannot be fully reversed, but effects are temporary.

For hyaluronic acid fillers, results can be partially or fully dissolved using hyaluronidase (Hylenex). This is one of the reasons we exclusively use HA fillers — the reversibility matters. If you leave your appointment unhappy, contact us immediately. We stand behind our work and will make it right.

Laser & Skin
Is laser hair removal permanent?

Laser hair removal is FDA-cleared for "permanent hair reduction," which means a significant and lasting reduction in hair count and regrowth density — not always a complete elimination of every follicle. Most patients achieve 80–90% reduction after a full series of 6–8 treatments, with periodic maintenance once or twice a year to address any regrowth.

Results depend on hair color, skin tone, the area being treated, and hormonal factors. The treatment works by targeting melanin in the hair follicle, so darker, coarser hair on lighter skin tends to respond most dramatically. We'll give you an honest assessment of what to expect for your specific situation during consultation.

What's a BBL and is it right for me?

BBL (BroadBand Light) is a form of intense pulsed light therapy that targets pigmentation irregularities, redness, sun damage, and early signs of aging at the surface level of the skin. It's not a laser — it delivers a broad spectrum of light that can be filtered for different targets. The results can be remarkable for patients with sun damage, rosacea, or uneven skin tone.

BBL works best on lighter skin tones and is not appropriate for all skin types. It involves minimal downtime — most patients experience some redness and darkening of pigmented spots for 7–10 days as the skin purges treated pigment. We offer complimentary BBL consultations and will assess your skin thoroughly before recommending treatment.

What's the downtime for a HydraFacial?

There is essentially no downtime with a HydraFacial. Most patients experience a healthy glow immediately after treatment, with no peeling, no sensitivity, and no restrictions on makeup or activity. It's one of the reasons it's so popular — you can schedule it the week of an event without worrying about how you'll look afterward.

The exception is if we combine it with a chemical peel booster, which can create some light flaking over the following 2–3 days. We'll always discuss exactly what to expect based on the specific protocol we're using for your skin.

Can I combine treatments in the same visit?

Often yes, and sometimes combining treatments amplifies results — for example, a HydraFacial followed by LED light therapy, or neuromodulators on the same day as a facial. However, some combinations require spacing: laser treatments and injectables, for instance, should generally not be done on the same day.

When you book a multi-treatment appointment, we'll sequence and space your services appropriately based on what's clinically safe and what will produce the best results. When in doubt, ask at consultation — we'll design a plan that makes sense.
