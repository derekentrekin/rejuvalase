01 · Foundation
Brand essence

Three words.

Everything — copy, color, layout, photography — should be measurable against these three words. If it fails any of them, it's not Rejuvalase.

01
Clinical

Medical authority, by design. Precise grids, high contrast, data where it matters. Never soft-pedal the expertise.

02
Editorial

Long serif headlines, generous whitespace, photography that could live in a magazine. Restrained, considered, never shouty.

03
Warm

Off-white paper tones, gold accent, first-name copy. Stafford's medspa, not a big-city franchise. People are the point.

04
Square

Per brand direction: every corner is 0px. No rounded buttons, no pilled tags, no fillet radii. The geometry is the identity.

×
Hard rule · zero radii

Buttons, cards, inputs, images, modals, badges — everything uses border-radius: 0. The only curved thing on the site is the serif's ball terminals. This is non-negotiable and applies everywhere.

Color usage rules

Gold — 10% of the page

Reserve gold for CTAs, italic accent words in headlines, prices, and the single most important micro-interaction. Never fill large blocks.

Ink 900 — structure

Ink carries typography, borders on interactive elements, and dark hero/CTA blocks. It should feel deliberate — not filler.

Paper — the stage

Paper is the default canvas. Leave most of it empty. Whitespace is the medspa's calmest and most luxurious material.

✓ Do
  • Use gold on CTAs, italic headline words, price, and the brand mark
  • Let paper (and whitespace) do most of the work
  • Pair gold with deep ink for maximum contrast
  • Limit functional colors to form validation states
× Don't
  • Use gold as a full-bleed background (it cheapens instantly)
  • Introduce new hues outside this palette — even "close" golds
  • Use the error red as a decorative color
  • Combine gold with rose-gold, copper, or any warm tone that confuses the primary

Typography rules

✓ Do
  • Italicize one or two words per headline in gold to add emphasis
  • Pair mono eyebrow above every serif headline — it's a signature
  • Keep line-height tight on display (0.95), generous on body (1.65)
  • Use hard line breaks in headlines to sculpt the ragged right edge
× Don't
  • Mix serifs — only Cormorant. No drop-in alternatives
  • Center long paragraphs of body copy
  • Use all-caps serif — mono handles all-caps duty
  • Exceed 72ch measure on body text

08 · Imagery
Photography direction

Editorial, not clinical-stock.

Every image should feel like it was shot in natural light for a lifestyle magazine — even the procedural ones. Avoid over-retouching, white-box medical photography, and beauty-industry clichés (rose petals, cucumber slices, stock portraits).

Image treatment

—
Natural light

Shot during golden hour or under large north windows. No ring lights. No clinical overheads.

—
Warm grade

Subtle warm tint in the mids, lifted shadows, never crushed. Skin stays textured.

—
Unposed

Mid-action moments — adjusting hair, laughing, mid-conversation. Eye contact is rare, intentional.

—
Local cast

Real Stafford clients wherever possible. Real interiors. No visible stock-photo markers.

Aspect ratios

Hero video, full-bleed

Portrait, team, product

Grid cards, social

Blog hero, features

✓ Do
  • Show texture — skin, linen, wood, marble
  • Favor asymmetric compositions with negative space
  • Shoot treatments in action, not static product placements
  • Crop tight on hands, tools, small moments
× Don't
  • Use stock photography of "beautiful women touching their face"
  • Retouch skin to plastic — visible pores = credibility
  • Include rose petals, cucumber slices, orchids, or lit candles
  • Shoot against pure white — use paper or linen backdrops
09 · Motion
Animation principles

Slow and considered.

Motion should feel like fabric, not spring-loaded plastic. No bounces. No overshoots. Most transitions use 180–320ms with a gentle cubic ease — fast enough to feel responsive, slow enough to feel intentional.

11 · Voice
Copy system

Three registers,
in even measure.

Rejuvalase copy sits at the intersection of three registers — each gets roughly equal weight across the site. Every page should demonstrate all three; every section leans into one.

Educational
33% · explainers, blog, FAQ

Clear, friendly, never condescending. Treats the reader as intelligent but curious. Explains the "why" before the "what."

"Neurotoxins like Botox relax the small muscles that cause expression lines — they don't fill anything. Fillers are the opposite: they add volume where the face has lost it."
"HydraFacial combines cleansing, exfoliation, extraction, and hydration into one 30-minute treatment. The vacuum tip is the reason you'll see immediate glow."
Aspirational
33% · hero, home, specials

Editorial, confident, a little poetic. Leans on serif and italicization. Suggests the outcome without promising it.

"Quiet luxury. Loud results."
"Not a new face. A clearer picture of the one you already have."
"The appointment you leave feeling, quietly, more like yourself."
Medical authority
33% · services, about, credentials

Precise, specific, evidence-forward. Uses clinical terminology correctly. Cites training, certifications, and numbers without being braggy.

"Every injection is performed by a licensed RN or MD with board-certified aesthetic training. We publish our injector CME hours annually."
"Sciton BBL uses broadband light across 420–1400nm to target pigmentation and redness at the dermal layer."

Voice rules

✓ Do
  • Use first-person "we" — we're a practice, not a platform
  • Name the city: "in Stafford" beats "in our area"
  • Use specific numbers (30 min, 1,200+ clients, 2019)
  • Italicize one word for emphasis, not three
  • Trust the serif to carry emotion — avoid exclamation points
× Don't
  • Use "reveal," "unveil," "glow up," "transform" — medspa clichés
  • Promise specific outcomes ("look 10 years younger")
  • Use "amazing," "incredible," "gorgeous" — adjective soup
  • Over-punctuate; the period is elegant, the exclamation is not
  • Talk down to readers about their own faces
